## 2026-10-03 — More levels and achievements for the new modes

Harry's request: "add more levels now that all the games are working well, and some achievements".

**Levels: 3 → 6 per new mode.** Classic's 11 levels are unchanged; its 5×5 path already fills the grid.

- *Minefield 4-6:* 6, 7 and 7 mines; minimum routes of 7, 6 and 7 steps; study time 9, 8 and 7 s.
  - A 4×4 board can't be harder through route length: 6 mines with an 8-step route (level 3) is the limit, with only 2 valid layouts.
  - So difficulty comes from more mines to remember and less study time.
  - Layouts were picked as the most scattered the validator allows, because a solid wall of mines is easy to remember. All 12 pass `node tools/minefield-layouts.js`.
- *Tone Pads 4-6:* tunes of 6→8, 7→10 and 8→11 notes, played faster each level.
- *Floor Is Lava 4-6:*
  - Flash 0.45 / 0.4 / 0.3 s and warning 1.9 / 1.8 / 1.8 s.
  - Escapes up to two tiles away, and levels 5-6 crumble.
  - An offline replay of the wave planner (5,000 runs per level) found no unfair waves and no extra forced FREEZE waves.

**Achievements: 5 per new mode.**

- *Where they're defined:* `Utils/AchievementDefs.js` is now the single list. The achievements screen and the unlock popup used to define the names separately; both now read this list.
- *The pattern for each mode:*
  - Clear level 1.
  - Clear level 3.
  - Clear every level.
  - Clear a level on the first try.
  - A mode special: Untouchable (every Minefield level on the first try), Long Memory (a 10-note tune), Survivor (50 lava waves).
- *Saving:* `SaveManager` keeps per-mode progress: highest level cleared, first-try clears, fails since the last clear, and stats. Old saves get an empty record per mode, with Classic untouched.
- *Hooks:* the modes report results through `ModeHelpers.recordLevelCleared` / `recordLevelFailed` / `recordBest` / `addToCount`.
- *Icons:* 15 new icons generated by `tools/make_achievement_icons.py`, in the Classic icons' neon style, and added to the achievements screen's Icon Textures list.
- *Testing:* checked in the Preview with a temporary test script, since removed. Tabs, locked and unlocked cards, and the unlock popup all work.

## 2026-10-03 — Playtest: Floor Is Lava feels too easy

**How it plays (Harry's playtest):** put on the glasses, select Floor Is Lava, stand in the middle. The game shows where the lava will land, and you step away from it. The next wave shows you again, and you step away again. "That doesn't even feel like a game." More play time won't fix this, because levels 2–3 change the rules but still show everything (see below).

**Why it's easy (from the code, `LavaController.js`):**

- *Full information.* The warning lights exactly which tiles will burn for the whole 2.0–2.4 s warning (`startWave` → `fx.setWarnTiles`). There is nothing to remember or work out.
- *One step, lots of time.* Every wave is planned around the tile you're standing on, with a guaranteed safe tile one step away (two on level 3), never behind you (`pickTarget`). A step takes about half a second, so a 2 s warning is very generous.
- *Many safe tiles:*
  - LINE burns only your row or column.
  - CHECKER leaves every orthogonal neighbour safe.
  - FREEZE means stand still.
  - Only ISLAND has a single safe tile, and on level 1 the cyan pillar points to it.
- *No pressure between waves.* The game waits until you settle on a tile before the next wave (`enterSettle`).
- *The core issue:* it's a reaction game with complete information inside a memory game. None of the other modes work that way.

**Candidate fixes (none decided or built):**

1. **Show, then hide.** Flash the warning briefly, then the board goes dark until the lava lands, so you must remember which tiles burn.
   - Small change: limit how long `setWarnTiles` stays lit; the ticks keep counting down.
   - Difficulty can scale through a shorter flash and more ISLAND waves.
   - A cheap device test of whether adding memory makes Lava fun, and its result would also inform Idea A.
2. **Queued waves.** Show the next two waves' patterns together, so the player plans a route through both from memory.
3. **Chained waves.** Drop the settle pause so waves come back-to-back from wherever the player ends up.
4. **Pure pressure.** Shorter warnings, more ISLAND waves, fewer LINE and FREEZE waves. This is harder but still not a memory game, and short reaction windows run into head-tracking latency. Not recommended on its own.
5. **Idea A** (object memory, below): the bigger redesign.

- [x] Prototype fix 1 (show, then hide), built 2026-10-03 in `LavaController.js`.
  - Each level has a `flash` time: 1.0 s on level 1 (which still shows the beacon during the flash), 0.7 s on level 2, 0.5 s on level 3.
  - After the flash, the warning and beacon fade out (`hideWarning`), and the countdown ticks carry on until the lava lands.

## 2026-10-03 — Gameplay ideas: object-memory lava and moving hazards (not implemented)

Documentation only: nothing below has been built, and no existing mode should change because of it.

### Where the modes stand

1. **Original Memory Grid / Path mode (Classic).** A grid appears on the floor and a safe path across it is shown for a short time. The path disappears, and the player has to walk the grid and reproduce the path from memory without stepping on the wrong tiles.
2. **Landmines (in game: Minefield).** Implemented; needs testing.
   - Mine locations are shown on the grid during a memorization phase, then disappear.
   - Unlike Path mode there is no single route to follow. The player walks whatever route they like to the goal, as long as they avoid the tiles that held mines.
   - In practice the player builds a mental note like "a mine near the front-left, two in the middle, one near the goal" and walks around those spots.
   - (Current build: 4×4 board.)
3. **Turnpads (in game: Tone Pads).** Implemented; needs testing.
   - The pads flash in a sequence, like a physical Simon game.
   - The player watches, then repeats the sequence by stepping on the right pads in the right order.
   - (Current build: four coloured pads around a centre home tile; each pad plays its own note.)
4. **The Floor Is Lava.** Implemented; needs testing. The current implementation stays as it is for now.
   - (Current build: 4×4 board in a lava moat. Amber tiles warn where lava will land, and the player reaches a safe tile before it does.)

### Idea A — Fall Guys-style object memory for The Floor Is Lava

Inspired by the Fall Guys fruit-memory round:

- Floor tiles show fruit, and players get time to memorize where each fruit is.
- The fruit disappears and the game calls out one fruit.
- Players have to stand on a tile that held it. Anyone in the wrong place falls when the wrong tiles drop away.

The Memory Grid version would use its own symbols, objects, icons or colours rather than copying Fall Guys. For example, the grid starts as:

```
🍓  🍌  🍎  🍌
🍎  🍓  🍇  🍎
🍌  🍇  🍓  🍇
🍇  🍎  🍌  🍓
```

1. The player studies the floor for a few seconds.
2. All the fruit disappears.
3. The game shows **STRAWBERRY**, and the player moves onto a tile they remember held a strawberry before the countdown ends.
4. At zero, the strawberry tiles stay safe and every other tile turns to lava. On a strawberry tile you survive; anywhere else you're burned.

The board does **not** have to show the fruit again straight away. The game can ask more questions about the same memorized layout, e.g. STRAWBERRY, then BANANA, then GRAPES. That forces the player to hold a map of the whole grid, not just one location. After several questions, a new arrangement is revealed and another memorization phase starts.

**Why it might fit.** It gives The Floor Is Lava a stronger memory-game identity, and it keeps the core Memory Grid loop of memorization plus physical movement while making the lava meaningful:

> look at the floor → memorize the object locations → objects disappear → the game asks for an object → move to where you remember it → the wrong tiles become lava

**Open decision (deliberately not made yet).** Choose later between:

1. Replacing / redesigning the existing Floor Is Lava gameplay with this mechanic.
2. Folding this mechanic into the existing Floor Is Lava gameplay without fully replacing it.
3. Keeping Floor Is Lava as it is and making this a separate mode.

**Possible difficulty progression (if pursued):**

- Start with only 2 different objects, then go up to 3, 4, 5 or more.
- Repeat the same object on several tiles.
- Shorten the memorization time.
- Shorten the time to reach the correct tile.
- Ask more questions before the full floor is shown again.
- Increase the grid's complexity.
- Ask for several different objects from the same layout.

*Easy round:* only apples and bananas, 8 seconds to memorize, the game asks for APPLE.

*Hard round:* strawberry, banana, apple, grapes, orange and watermelon spread across the grid, only a few seconds to memorize. Then GRAPES, then WATERMELON, then BANANA, with no full reveal in between.

The pressure comes from holding a mental map of many objects at once, which is what makes the Fall Guys round interesting.

### Idea B — Moving hazards and shifting safe zones (experimental)

Inspired by an interactive-floor video seen on LinkedIn, where the floor is far more active than any current Memory Grid mode:

- There's a safe area the player must reach, and then it moves somewhere else.
- Meanwhile a red danger section sweeps across the play area.
- The player reacts physically: steps out of the hazard's path, or possibly hops over it, then heads for the next safe area.

**Possible variations:**

- A red line sweeping horizontally or vertically across the grid.
- A hazard travelling from one side of the grid to the other.
- Multiple moving hazard lines.
- A rotating hazard.
- Safe zones that change position.
- Smaller safe zones as difficulty rises.
- Faster hazards.
- A low sweeping hazard the player has to hop over.
- Moving toward a destination while avoiding hazards on the way.

**It may become** a separate mode, a variation or modifier of another mode, or something we decide doesn't fit Memory Grid.

**Technical questions to answer only if we decide to prototype it:**

- How accurately can we tell which floor tile the player actually occupies?
- How should player position be inferred from head tracking? Do we need an estimate of body or foot position?
- How reliable would jump / hop detection be?
- How should collision with a moving floor hazard be decided? Could the hazard occupy grid tiles logically instead of needing literal body collision?
- How do we keep the required movements safe and reasonable inside the play area?

(Related groundwork already in the code, for when this comes up: `ModeHelpers.bodyLocal` estimates body position from the head pose, and Floor Is Lava only confirms a tile after the head holds near its centre briefly.)

### Feasibility against the current code (assessed 2026-10-03, nothing built)

**Idea A: very feasible, medium effort.** It mostly reuses Floor Is Lava's existing wave cycle (plan → warn → land → judge → cool, `LavaController.js` `startWave` … `cool`). A question is a wave whose safe tiles are the ones that held the requested object, with a warning phase that shows no hint.

- *Reusable as is:*
  - Tile occupancy and judging: a tile is confirmed after the head holds within 21 cm of its centre for 0.3 s, plus a 0.4 s landing grace (`isSafeNow`). Already proven on device with 55 cm tiles.
  - The moat, burn effects, level restart, crumbled tiles and the HUD.
  - Studying from outside the board: Minefield's start marker, where stepping on ends the study.
  - Symbol textures: Python-generated like `tools/make_menu_icons.py`, drawn as quads the way `MenuCards.js` draws its icons.
  - Level tables like `LEVELS`, and offline fairness checks like `tools/minefield-layouts.js`.
- *New work:*
  - A symbol set, floor icon visuals, and a reveal/hide animation.
  - A layout generator: object count, repeats, spread.
  - A question picker: never the player's current tile, and at least one answer reachable within the countdown.
  - MEMORIZE and QUESTION phases.
  - A way to show the requested object: text, or an icon on a head-following panel like Tone Pads' mini board.
- *Risks to test early:*
  - Symbol readability on the additive display, outdoors and for colour-blind players: give each object its own shape as well as its own colour, and draw our own symbols rather than emoji images.
  - The narrow field of view: the 2.2 m board can't be seen at once, so memorizing becomes scanning. Tune the memorize time on device.
- *Cost of each option:*
  - Replace: least plumbing; it stays in `LavaController.js` and the menu card's rule text changes.
  - Mix in as a new wave type: moderate; it needs a memorize phase, and the warning must hide the answer.
  - Separate mode: most work outside the gameplay. The menu is a 2×2 grid of four cards, so a fifth mode means a menu redesign, an achievements tab and a level tag.

**Idea B: depends on the version.**

- *Tile-based hazards* (rows/columns stepping across on a beat): feasible, small to medium.
  - It's close to Lava's LINE waves.
  - The cyan beacon (`fx.setSafe`) can move between waves to mark the next safe zone.
  - A sweeping bar can reuse the glowing curtain and scrolling stripes from `BoardBorder.js`.
  - Design catch: on a 4×4 board, a full-width sweep can't be dodged without leaving the board or jumping. It needs gaps, partial lines, or sweeps from two sides.
- *Continuous hazard with body collision:* hard and likely to feel unfair.
  - Only the head is tracked. `ModeHelpers.bodyLocal` gives one estimated point, off by roughly 10–20 cm, with no foot positions.
  - Letting the hazard occupy tiles is more honest.
- *Jump detection:* possible to try, reliability unknown.
  - Head height above the floor is available; Minefield uses it (`MIN_HEAD_HEIGHT`).
  - But walking bob and crouching also move the head, and jumping in glasses is a safety risk, especially on uneven ground.
  - If tried, keep it optional: a hazard that can also be stepped around.
- *Perception:* with the narrow field of view, a hazard at the player's feet is usually out of sight. Real-time dodging needs telegraphing and audio cues.
- *Safety:* keep Lava's rule that a safe tile is never behind the player.

**Suggested order:**

1. Prototype Idea A as a wave type or level variant inside Floor Is Lava. That's the cheapest device test before choosing replace, mix-in or separate mode.
2. Try Idea B's tile-based sweep as an experimental Lava wave.
3. Leave continuous collision and jumping for last.
